How it works — the full method, no black box

  1. Estimate uncertainty. Each WBS line has a Min, Most likely and Max cost. In every iteration the tool draws one value from a triangular distribution between these three points.
  2. Risk events. Only risks with Status = Open are used. In every iteration each risk either happens (with its probability) or not. If it happens, its cost impact and schedule delay are drawn from their own triangular distributions. The same occurrence hits cost and schedule.
  3. Total cost = sum of the WBS draws + cost of the risks that happened.
    Budget (BAC) = sum of the Most likely values + contingency in budget. Contingency itself is not simulated — the question is whether it is enough.
  4. Finish date = planned finish + remaining-duration uncertainty (triangular, working days) + delays of risks that happened − schedule contingency, counted in working days (Mon–Fri). All risk delays are assumed to hit the critical path and add up, which is conservative.
  5. Results. After all iterations the tool sorts the outcomes: P50 is the value with a 50% chance of not being exceeded, P80 with 80%, and so on. Contingency needed for P80 = P80 cost − base estimate.
  6. Drivers. Each risk is ranked by Spearman rank correlation between its sampled impact and the total cost (or finish delay). The top drivers are where mitigation pays off most.

Good to know

  • The same inputs and random seed always give the same result, so runs are reproducible and auditable.
  • Cost lines and risks are treated as independent. In real projects they are often correlated, so the true range can be wider.
  • Results are only as good as the three-point estimates and probabilities in the register. Use the tool to start a conversation, not to replace judgement.
